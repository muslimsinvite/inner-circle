Section III · Verses 4–8 · Moral Architecture

From Ahsan Taqweem to Asfala Safileen—and the Unbroken Exception

Having sworn by the three prophetic sanctuaries, Allah unveils the central thesis (Jawab al-Qasam): the majestic elevation of human design, the peril of its collapse, and the sole path that preserves human worth. Explore this arc across three classical exegetical lenses.

Verse 95:7 · The Moral Challenge
فَمَا يُكَذِّبُكَ بَعْدُ بِالدِّينِ

“So what yet causes you to deny the Recompense?”

Notice the shift to direct second-person address (yukadhdhibuka): after observing the brilliance of human creation alongside the reality of moral decline, denying ad-Din (the Day of Moral Reckoning) becomes intellectually indefensible.

Verse 95:8 · The Crowning Seal
أَلَيْسَ اللَّهُ بِأَحْكَمِ الْحَاكِمِينَ

“Is not Allah the most just of judges?”

The superlative Ahkam al-Hakimeen unites wisdom (hikmah) with impartial judgment (hukm). Because human actions produce divergent moral outcomes in verses 4–6, divine justice necessitates a final court where every deed receives its exact measure.
