Surah Al-Anbiya: The Prophets and the Approaching Account

Al-Anbiya — Chapter 21 (Verses 1–112)

Opening with the urgent warning that humanity’s reckoning has drawn near while hearts remain distracted, Surah Al-Anbiya unites cosmic signs, historical deliverance, and sixteen prophetic figures around one central truth: every messenger was human, every revelation carried the same monotheistic call, and creation culminates in justice and mercy.

How the 112 verses unfold across four interlocking movements

Movement I · Verses 1–15

Revelation arrives while hearts are at play

The surah begins with a sharp psychological diagnosis: the reckoning is near, yet people receive fresh revelation with distracted hearts (لَاهِيَةً قُلُوبُهُمْ). Instead of reflecting on the Book that holds their own honorable mention (21:10), opponents whisper private dismissals—all heard by the Knower of heaven and earth.

Movement II · Verses 16–47

Creation is purposeful, ordered, and weighed to a mustard seed

Just as listeners must not treat revelation as a game, Allah did not create heaven and earth in play (21:16). Truth is hurled against falsehood until falsehood vanishes (21:18). Explore the six cosmic and rational proofs that anchor monotheism and culminate in the Scales of Justice (21:47).

Movement III · Verses 48–93

Seventeen prophetic figures & signs united in one religion

The central 46 verses give the chapter its name. Rather than distant legends, these prophets and righteous figures are presented as human servants tested by idolatry, tyranny, flood, illness, isolation, and sorrow—all calling upon the same Lord and uniting in verse 92: “Indeed this, your religion, is one religion.”

Structural Pattern · “Fastajabna lahu” (So We responded to him)

Four prayers in distress, one unfailing divine response

Noah · vv. 76–77

Deliverance from the Great Flood

“When he called before that time, so We responded to him and saved him and his family from the great flood.”

فَاسْتَجَبْنَا لَهُ فَنَجَّيْنَاهُ وَأَهْلَهُ مِنَ الْكَرْبِ الْعَظِيمِ

Job · vv. 83–84

Mercy Over Prolonged Adversity

“Indeed, adversity has touched me, and You are the Most Merciful of the merciful.” Removed harm and doubled his family.

أَنِّي مَسَّنِيَ الضُّرُّ وَأَنْتَ أَرْحَمُ الرَّاحِمِينَ

Jonah · vv. 87–88

The Call Within the Darknesses

“There is no deity except You; exalted are You. Indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers.” “And thus do We save the believers.”

لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ سُبْحَانَكَ إِنِّي كُنْتُ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

Zechariah · vv. 89–90

Hope, Fear, and the Gift of John

“My Lord, do not leave me alone, while You are the best of inheritors.” Hastened to good deeds in hope and fear.

رَبِّ لَا تَذَرْنِي فَرْدًا وَأَنْتَ خَيْرُ الْوَارِثِينَ

Movement IV · Verses 94–112

The folded sky, righteous inheritance, and mercy to the worlds

The final movement closes the circle opened in verse 1. Every righteous effort is recorded (21:94), the barrier of Gog and Magog gives way before the true promise (21:96–97), the sky is folded like a written scroll (21:104), and the mission of Muhammad is defined in four luminous words: mercy to the worlds (21:107).

Verses 96–100 · The Regret of Heedlessness

Staring in horror when the promise arrives

When the true promise nears, those who ignored the signs confess: “O woe to us; we had been unmindful of this; rather, we were wrongdoers” (21:97). False deities and their worshippers share the same fuel of Hell—proving those idols never possessed divinity (21:99).

Verses 101–107 · The Promise to the Righteous

Far removed from fear, met by angels

Those for whom the best reward preceded do not even hear Hell’s sound, nor are they grieved by the greatest terror (21:101–103). Written already in the Psalms (az-Zabur): “the land is inherited by My righteous servants” (21:105).
