03 · Lexical Mastery & Moral Polarity

From Istaghna to Wasjud Waqtarib: Eight Defining Terms

The moral diagnosis of Surah Al-‘Alaq hinges on precise Arabic contrasts: biological dependence (‘alaq) versus imagined self-sufficiency (istaghna), and a lying forelock (nasiyah) versus the lowliest posture of worship (wasjud) that achieves the highest nearness (waqtarib).

Dimension Path of Self-Sufficiency (Istaghna, vv. 6–18) Path of Servanthood & Nearness (‘Abd & Sujud, vv. 1–5, 10–12, 19)
Origin & Self-View Sees himself self-sufficient (ra’ahu istaghna, v. 7), forgetting his origin from a clinging substance (‘alaq, v. 2). Acknowledges creation by the Lord (v. 1) and stands humbly as a servant when praying (‘abdan idha salla, v. 10).
Attitude Toward Truth Denies and turns away (kadhdhaba wa tawalla, v. 13), speaking from a lying, sinning forelock (v. 16). Recites in the Lord’s name (v. 1), learns what was unknown via the pen (vv. 4–5), and walks upon guidance (v. 11).
Action Toward Others Transgresses bounds (yatgha, v. 6) and actively forbids a servant from prayer (vv. 9–10). Prays consistently and enjoins righteousness / God-consciousness (amara bi-t-taqwa, v. 12).
Awareness of Return Forgets that to the Lord is the return (ar-ruj‘a, v. 8) and acts as if Allah does not see (v. 14). Refuses obedience to the tyrant (la tuti‘hu) and anchors life in Allah’s sight and ultimate return (vv. 8, 14, 19).
Allies & Outcome Relies on earthly associates (nadiyahu, v. 17) while summoned to face the angels of Hell (az-zabaniyah, v. 18). Answered by the Most Generous Lord (Al-Akram, v. 3); achieves closeness through prostration (wasjud waqtarib, v. 19).
