Surah Al-Qāri'ah: The Striking Calamity

Eleven verses arranged in three tightening movements: an acoustic shock that shakes human certainty, two cosmic similes that dissolve physical solidity into weightless dust, and the moral scales (Mawāzīn) where human destiny is weighed.

Thematic Architecture

How Three Movements Build an Unbroken Moral Argument

Surah Al-Qāri'ah does not merely describe the Last Day—it uses an echoing question-and-answer frame to turn external cosmic collapse inward toward personal accountability. Switch lenses below to trace its architecture.

Cosmic Imagery · Verses 4 & 5

When Mountains Become Wool and Crowds Scatter Like Moths

Move the slider below from ordinary earthly stability to the Day of Al-Qāri'ah. Watch how both pillars of human confidence—social order and geological permanence—lose coherence at the exact same moment.

Verse 101:4 · Human Bewilderment

يَوْمَ يَكُونُ النَّاسُ كَالْفَرَاشِ الْمَبْثُوثِ

People Like Dispersed Moths (Al-Farāsh al-Mabthūth)

Farāsh evokes small winged insects surging erratically toward light, colliding without leadership or rank. Mabthūth means scattered far and wide—signifying the sudden collapse of earthly tribes, hierarchies, and deliberate plans.

Verse 101:5 · Physical Weightlessness

وَتَكُونُ الْجِبَالُ كَالْعِهْنِ الْمَنْفُوشِ

Mountains Like Carded Wool (Al-'Ihn al-Manfūsh)

'Ihn refers specifically to dyed wool, while manfūsh describes fibers teased and fluffed apart by hand or comb until air passes freely through them. What once anchored the earth now floats without mass—preparing the paradox of the next verses, where only moral deeds possess weight.

The Doctrine of Mawāzīn · Verses 6–9

Moral Gravity: The Scales That Determine Human Destiny

After physical mountains become weightless wool, the surah introduces the plural Mawāzīn (scales). Adjust the balance below to explore how the text frames the two decisive outcomes.

Heavy Scales — Thaqulat Mawāzīnuhu (vv. 6–7) “Then as for one whose scales are heavy [with good deeds], he will be in a pleasant life.”
Verses 6–7 · Heavy Scales

فَأَمَّا مَنْ ثَقُلَتْ مَوَازِينُهُ ﴿٦﴾ فَهُوَ فِي عِيشَةٍ رَاضِيَةٍ ﴿٧﴾

A Life That Is Itself Contented ('Īshah Rāḍiyah)

Notice the active participle rāḍiyah ("pleased / satisfied"): rather than merely saying the person is pleased, the Quran describes the life itself ('īshah) as radiating satisfaction—meaning harmony without regret, deficiency, or fear.

Verses 8–9 · Light Scales

وَأَمَّا مَنْ خَفَّتْ مَوَازِينُهُ ﴿٨﴾ فَأُمُّهُ هَاوِيَةٌ ﴿٩﴾

Weightlessness Leading to the Abyss (Fa-ummuhu Hāwiyah)

In verse 8, deeds are not weighed by quantity alone but by spiritual gravity. Those whose scales rise light lose the shelter of permanence, and their sole "refuge" (umm) becomes an abyss.
