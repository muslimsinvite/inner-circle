Across twenty-eight concise verses, it maps an exhaustive campaign of night-and-day persuasion, the weaving of celestial and earthly signs into moral proof, the sociological machinery of ancestral idolatry, and a closing supplication that balances decisive justice with universal mercy.

Movement I · Verses 71:1–71:12

Tireless outreach shifted from public proclamation to intimate private counsel

The surah opens with an urgent mandate: Nuh is sent to warn his people before a painful punishment arrives (71:1). His opening address in 71:2–4 establishes three interlocking duties — worship Allah, be conscious of Him (ittaqūh), and obey His messenger — coupled with a profound incentive: forgiveness of sins and a reprieve until a designated term (ajal musammā). Yet once Allah’s appointed term arrives, no delay is possible.

Beginning in verse 71:5, the perspective turns upward into intimate prayer. Nuh reports not a single method, but an entire spectrum of communicative strategies deployed relentlessly across time and social space. Notice the psychological anatomy of refusal in verses 71:6–7: every invitation to receive forgiveness triggered a physical and emotional barricade.

71:3–4 · The Core Summons Commission

أَنِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ وَاتَّقُوهُ وَأَطِيعُونِ ﴿٣﴾ يَغْفِرْ لَكُمْ مِنْ ذُنُوبِكُمْ وَيُؤَخِّرْكُمْ إِلَى أَجَلٍ مُسَمًّى ۚ إِنَّ أَجَلَ اللَّهِ إِذَا جَاءَ لَا يُؤَخَّرُ لَوْ كُنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ

“[Saying], ‘Worship Allah, fear Him and obey me. Allah will forgive you of your sins and delay you for a specified term. Indeed, the time [set by] Allah, when it comes, will not be delayed, if you only knew.’”

Movement II · Verses 71:13–71:20

Nuh anchors theology in observable signs — from layered heavens to earthly passage

At verse 71:13, Nuh shifts from promises of blessing to an incisive rhetorical question: “What is the matter with you that you do not attribute to Allah due grandeur (waqār)?” To dismantle their indifference, he constructs a seamless vertical panorama spanning human embryology and lifespan, the architecture of the cosmos, and the habitable geometry of the earth.

Movement III · Verses 71:21–71:25

Elite wealth-worship and conspiracy rallied around five ancestral idols

In verse 71:21, Nuh identifies the social structure driving disobedience: the masses followed elites “whose wealth and children will not increase him except in loss.” The wording intentionally echoes and inverts verse 71:12. Where repentance turns wealth and offspring into divine blessing, arrogance converts those very assets into spiritual liability.

Rather than passive skepticism, the leadership engineered makran kubbārā — an immense, coordinated conspiracy (71:22). Their rallying cry in 71:23 doubles the emphatic prohibition (lā tadharunna — “never leave!”) to bind communal identity to five named deities, ensuring resistance became an ideological fortress.

71:21–22 · The Wealth Inversion Sociology

قَالَ نُوحٌ رَبِّ إِنَّهُمْ عَصَوْنِي وَاتَّبَعُوا مَنْ لَمْ يَزِدْهُ مَالُهُ وَوَلَدُهُ إِلَّا خَسَارًا ﴿٢١﴾ وَمَكَرُوا مَكْرًا كُبَّارًا

Noah said, “My Lord, indeed they have disobeyed me and followed him whose wealth and children will not increase him except in loss. And they conspired an immense conspiracy.”

The Five Idols Named in Verse 71:23

71:25 · Immediate Retribution

Drowned and Entered into the Fire

مِمَّا خَطِيئَاتِهِمْ أُغْرِقُوا فَأُدْخِلُوا نَارًا فَلَمْ يَجِدُوا لَهُمْ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ أَنْصَارًا

Notice the swift Arabic conjunction fa- (ughriqū fa-udkhilū nārā): “Because of their sins they were drowned and put into the Fire, and they found not for themselves besides Allah any helpers.” None of the five revered idols or wealthy patrons could mount a rescue when the appointed term arrived.

Movement IV · Verses 71:26–71:28

Exhausted by generational corruption, Nuh closes with decisive justice and sweeping mercy

71:26–27 · Justice & Moral Realism

Why Nuh Prayed Against Any Remnant

وَقَالَ نُوحٌ رَبِّ لَا تَذَرْ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ مِنَ الْكَافِرِينَ دَيَّارًا ﴿٢٦﴾ إِنَّكَ إِنْ تَذَرْهُمْ يُضِلُّوا عِبَادَكَ وَلَا يَلِدُوا إِلَّا فَاجِرًا كَفَّارًا

Nuh’s prayer in 71:26 is grounded in two explicit reasons given in 71:27: first, left unchecked, they will actively mislead Allah’s faithful servants (yuḍillū ‘ibādaka); second, their entrenched corruption reproduces itself across generations (fājiran kaffārā). The plea comes only after exhausting night-and-day, public, and private appeals.

71:28 · The Expanding Du’a

From Personal Pardon to All Believers

رَبِّ اغْفِرْ لِي وَلِوَالِدَيَّ وَلِمَنْ دَخَلَ بَيْتِيَ مُؤْمِنًا وَلِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتِ وَلَا تَزِدِ الظَّالِمِينَ إِلَّا تَبَارًا

Even at the climax of judgment, Nuh opens his final breath with forgiveness (Rabbi-ghfir lī), widening outward in four concentric circles before sealing the surah’s symmetry against wrongdoing.
