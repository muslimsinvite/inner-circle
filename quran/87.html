Section III · Linguistic & Rhetorical Insights

Parallel verb pairs and a unified -ā cadence bind the text

Every verse in Surah Al-A‘la closes with the open long vowel -ā. Eighteen verses terminate with a curled alif maqṣūrah (ى), while verse 87:16 (الدُّنْيَا) shifts orthographically to a vertical alif (ا) right where the Surah indicts worldly preference.

Section IV · Theological Synthesis

Two human trajectories diverge over remembrance and permanence

At the center of Surah Al-A‘la stands a decisive moral fork: the reminder goes out universally, yet human response splits along inner awe versus avoidance and short-term worldly preference.

Path of Success · 87:10, 14–15, 17

He Who Fears & Purifies Himself

  • Inner Disposition (87:10): Possesses reverent awe (man yakhshā) and therefore receives the reminder (sayadhdhakkaru).
  • Purification (87:14): Actively purifies soul, conduct, and intention (tazakkā), earning certainty of success (qad aflaḥa).
  • Devotion (87:15): Mentions/remembers the name of his Lord (dhakara isma rabbihi) and establishes prayer (fa-ṣallā).
  • Valuation (87:17): Prioritizes the Hereafter, which is both qualitatively better (khayrun) and enduring (wa-abqā).
Path of Ruin · 87:11–13, 16

The Wretched One Who Avoids It

  • Inner Disposition (87:11): The most wretched (al-ashqā) deliberately turns aside and avoids the reminder (yatajannabuhā).
  • Misplaced Priority (87:16): Prefers the immediate, lower worldly life (tu’thirūna l-ḥayāta d-dunyā) despite its stubble-like transience.
  • Consequence (87:12): Enters and burns in the greatest Fire (yaṣlā n-nāra l-kubrā).
  • Suspended State (87:13): Neither dies therein to find relief nor truly lives (lā yamūtu fīhā wa-lā yaḥyā).
Principle 01 · 87:1–3

Transcendence Joined to Purpose

Allah is Al-A‘lā—above all defect or rival—yet His transcendence is expressed through intimate order: creating, proportioning, measuring destiny, and guiding every creature.

Principle 02 · 87:4–5 & 16–17

Impermanence as Moral Mirror

Pasture turning into black stubble is not merely botanical observation; it is an embodied argument against trading an enduring Hereafter for a perishable worldly life.

Principle 03 · 87:6–8

Safeguarded Revelation & Ease

Preservation of the recited revelation rests on Allah’s promise and omniscience over the overt and hidden, paired with facilitating the path of ease (al-yusrā).

Principle 04 · 87:9–11

Moral Accountability of Listening

Prophetic reminding is a duty, yet benefit requires a receptive heart. Awe opens the listener to transformation; arrogance or avoidance seals the warning out.

Principle 05 · 87:14–15

Integrated Success (Falāḥ)

Success is not passive belief alone: it integrates self-purification (tazakkā), conscious remembrance of Allah’s name, and embodied prayer (ṣallā).

Principle 06 · 87:18–19

Continuity of Prophetic Scriptures

The closing verses anchor these ethical truths in universal prophetic heritage—explicitly citing the former scriptures of Abraham (Ibrāhīm) and Moses (Mūsā).
