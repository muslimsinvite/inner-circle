The Night of Decree

A Study & Mastery Guide to Surah Al-Qadr

In five compressed verses, Surah Al-Qadr traces the arc of a single night from the sending down of the Quran, through an unanswerable question of awe and a measure exceeding a thousand months, to a cosmic descent that settles into unbroken peace until dawn.

02 · TEMPORAL MAGNITUDE (97:2–97:3)

A single night outweighs 83 years and 4 months of ordinary time

Verse 97:2 asks, “And what can make you know what is the Night of Decree?”—signaling that its worth lies beyond ordinary intuition. Verse 97:3 then anchors that worth with a concrete benchmark: “better than a thousand months.”

TEXTUAL PRECISION

Notice that 97:3 does not say “equal to” a thousand months—it states khayrun min (“better than” a thousand months). The figure is a lower bound, not a ceiling.

03 · LITERARY ARCHITECTURE

Repetition names the night three times before yielding to pronouns of presence

Inspect how Surah Al-Qadr shifts from third-person majesty and explicit naming in verses 1–3 to continuous vertical movement and serene closure in verses 4–5. Select a structural lens to trace its thread across all five verses.

LENS ACTIVE: NAMING → PRONOUN SHIFT Verses 1–5
STRUCTURAL INTERPRETATION

Three explicit namings anchor the mystery before pronouns carry it

In verses 1, 2, and 3, the full construct phrase “Laylat al-Qadr” (Night of Decree) is repeated verbatim three times. Once its rank is established as better than a thousand months, verses 4 and 5 drop the noun phrase and shift to feminine pronouns (“fīhā” therein, “hiya” it is)—reflecting total familiarity and presence.
