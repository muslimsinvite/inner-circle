Section 03 · The Opening Oaths (85:1–85:3)

Three oaths summon a cosmic courtroom before the trench is ever mentioned

Classical Quranic oaths (qasam) establish grandeur and accountability before delivering their verdict (jawab al-qasam). Here, the sky, the Promised Day, and the act of witnessing directly answer the tyrants' illusion of secrecy and impunity.

85:1 Al-Sama' dhat al-Buruj

وَالسَّمَاءِ ذَاتِ الْبُرُوجِ

By the sky containing great stars

The noun بُرُوج (buruj, plural of burj) shares its root with lofty, fortified towers. Exegetes explain it as the grand constellations, zodiacal mansions through which celestial bodies travel, or luminous star-towers that serve as signs of order—contrasting the vast open heavens with the cramped earthly ditch below.

85:2 Al-Yawm al-Maw'ud

وَالْيَوْمِ الْمَوْعُودِ

And [by] the Promised Day

Al-Yawm al-Maw'ud refers unanimously to the Day of Resurrection—the appointed court date where historical injustice is reopened. By swearing by it immediately after the stars, the Surah anchors earthly suffering inside an unavoidable cosmic horizon of justice.

85:3 Shahid wa Mashhud

وَشَاهِدٍ وَمَشْهُودٍ

And [by] the witness and what is witnessed

Left intentionally indefinite to encompass every observer and observed scene—traditionally linked to Friday (shahid) and the Day of Arafah (mashhud), the Prophet and his community, or Allah and all deeds. Linguistically, it plants the root Sh-H-D that reverberates through verses 7 and 9.

Section 04 · Historical & Scriptural Context

The People of the Trench (Ashab al-Ukhdud) and the anatomy of persecution

Verses 4–9 pivot abruptly from the heavens to a burning pit: "Cursed were the companions of the trench" (qutila ashab al-ukhdud). Explore the historical traditions and why the Quran leaves the believers' sole "offense" crystal clear in verse 8.

Verse 85:8 · The Sole Grievance

وَمَا نَقَمُوا مِنْهُمْ إِلَّا أَنْ يُؤْمِنُوا بِاللَّهِ الْعَزِيزِ الْحَمِيدِ

"And they resented them not except because they believed in Allah, the Exalted in Might, the Praiseworthy."

No Political Crime The restrictive phrasing (مَا... إِلَّا) strips away every pretext: the victims committed no rebellion or harm—their faith itself was treated as intolerable.
Why "Al-Aziz Al-Hamid"? Al-Aziz (Exalted in Might) refutes the persecutors' apparent strength; Al-Hamid (Praiseworthy) affirms that God remains worthy of praise even in the hour of trial.
Meccan Resonance Revealed in Mecca while early Muslims endured boycotts and torture, the Surah reminds them that steadfastness against tyranny is an ancient prophetic legacy.
Section 05 · Divine Retribution & Enduring Sovereignty (85:10–85:20)

Severe accountability paired with an open door of repentance and seven divine attributes

Notice the astonishing moral balance in verses 10–16: even perpetrators who burned believers alive are invited to repent before judgment, while the Divine Names move seamlessly from severe retribution (batsh) to forgiving affection (Al-Ghafur Al-Wadud).

85:10 · Warning & Repentance Clause

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ فَتَنُوا الْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتِ ثُمَّ لَمْ يَتُوبُوا فَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ جَهَنَّمَ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ الْحَرِيقِ

"Indeed, those who have tortured the believing men and believing women and then have not repented will have the punishment of Hell, and they will have the punishment of the Burning Fire."

Notice two details: both men and women (al-mu'minina wa-l-mu'minat) are named explicitly, and the phrase "and then have not repented" (thumma lam yatubu) keeps mercy open even for persecutors—yet if unrepented, their earthly fire (nar dhat al-waqud) is answered in kind by 'adhab al-hariq (the Burning Fire).

85:11 · The Great Attainment

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ لَهُمْ جَنَّاتٌ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ ذَٰلِكَ الْفَوْزُ الْكَبِيرُ

"Indeed, those who have believed and done righteous deeds will have gardens beneath which rivers flow. That is the great attainment."

Where tyrants dug a sunken trench filled with blazing fuel (85:4–5), believers inherit lush gardens under which cool rivers flow. What looked like total defeat beside the pit is reframed as al-fawz al-kabir—the supreme, irreversible victory.

Section 06 · The Closing Seal (85:21–85:22)

The Preserved Slate (Lawh Mahfuz) and five enduring takeaways

After exposing the persistent denial (takdhib, 85:19) of disbelievers and reminding them that Allah encompasses them from behind (muhit, 85:20), the Surah delivers its final seal: the message they mock does not belong to fragile parchment—it stands inscribed in a guarded celestial register.

85:21–85:22 Bal huwa Quranun Majid · Fi Lawhin Mahfuz

بَلْ هُوَ قُرْآنٌ مَجِيدٌ ۝ فِي لَوْحٍ مَحْفُوظٍ

"But this is an honored Quran, [inscribed] in a Preserved Slate."

The adjective مَجِيدٌ (Majid — glorious, noble, vast in honor) appears only twice in this Surah: once describing God's Throne (85:15) and once describing the Quran (85:21). Because its source is the Owner of the Throne and its record is the Lawh Mahfuz (Preserved Slate), neither political coercion nor mockery can distort or extinguish its truth.

TAKEAWAY 01 · WITNESSING

No act of cruelty occurs off-record

Persecutors may sit calmly as witnesses (shuhud, 85:7), yet Allah is the encompassing Witness (Shahid, 85:9) and the Promised Day (85:2) brings every hidden deed into open light.

TAKEAWAY 02 · REDEFINING VICTORY

Steadfast faith outlasts earthly power

Dying in fidelity beside a trench is not failure; Surah Al-Buruj names eternal gardens with flowing rivers al-fawz al-kabir—the great attainment (85:11).

TAKEAWAY 03 · JUSTICE & AFFECTION

Divine severity never cancels divine love

Verse 10 extends repentance even to torturers, and verses 12–14 pair severe retribution (batsh) with Al-Ghafur Al-Wadud—Forgiving and deeply Affectionate toward the faithful.

TAKEAWAY 04 · ENCIRCLEMENT

Empires are surrounded from behind

Invoking Pharaoh and Thamud (85:17–18) reminds deniers that superior armies never protect against the Creator who encompasses them from unseen angles (muhit, 85:20).

TAKEAWAY 05 · PRESERVATION

Truth rests in the Preserved Slate

The Quran is guarded in the Lawh Mahfuz (85:22), immune to alteration, forgetfulness, or the persistent denial of its contemporaries.
