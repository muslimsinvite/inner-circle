Movement I · Verses 58:1–58:4

When a Woman’s Complaint Abolished Zihar

Surah Al-Mujadila opens not with an abstract legal treatise, but with an urgent human voice. Khawlah bint Tha‘labah came to the Prophet Muhammad arguing concerning her husband and directing her complaint directly to God. In classical pre-Islamic custom, her husband had pronounced zihar—a declaration equating his wife’s status to that of his mother’s back—which left a woman suspended in limbo: neither cherished as a wife nor freed through divorce to remarry.

Verse 58:1 validates her persistence with extraordinary tenderness: “Certainly has Allah heard the speech of the one who argues with you concerning her husband and directs her complaint to Allah.” Notice the rare word tahawurakuma (“your dialogue”): Allah records that He is actively listening to their back-and-forth conversation, closing with His twin attributes of Hearing and Seeing (Sami‘un Basir).

Verse 58:2 then dismantles the legal fiction of zihar at its root. Calling a wife a “mother” does not alter biological or sacred reality—“Their mothers are none but those who gave birth to them.” The verse strips zihar of marital dissolution power, denounces it as an objectionable statement and a falsehood (munkaran min al-qawli wa zura), and opens the door to repair by reminding believers that Allah is Pardoning and Forgiving (‘Afuwwun Ghafur).

Why does 58:2 mention “Pardoning and Forgiving” after condemning zihar?

Because the Surah seeks restoration rather than permanent rupture. By declaring the utterance morally offensive yet non-terminating, revelation rebukes reckless speech while giving repentant husbands a structured path back to lawful marriage through tangible responsibility.

Verse 58:1 · The Hearing of the Plea Sami‘un Basir

قَدْ سَمِعَ اللَّهُ قَوْلَ الَّتِي تُجَادِلُكَ فِي زَوْجِهَا وَتَشْتَكِي إِلَى اللَّهِ وَاللَّهُ يَسْمَعُ تَحَاوُرَكُمَا ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ سَمِيعٌ بَصِيرٌ

Certainly has Allah heard the speech of the one who argues with you, [O Muhammad], concerning her husband and directs her complaint to Allah. And Allah hears your dialogue; indeed, Allah is Hearing and Seeing.

Verse 58:2 · Legal Abolition of Zihar Munkar & Zur

الَّذِينَ يُظَاهِرُونَ مِنْكُمْ مِنْ نِسَائِهِمْ مَا هُنَّ أُمَّهَاتِهِمْ ۖ إِنْ أُمَّهَاتُهُمْ إِلَّا اللَّائِي وَلَدْنَهُمْ ۚ وَإِنَّهُمْ لَيَقُولُونَ مُنْكَرًا مِنَ الْقَوْلِ وَزُورًا ۚ وَإِنَّ اللَّهَ لَعَفُوٌّ غَفُورٌ

Those who pronounce thihar among you [to separate] from their wives — they are not [consequently] their mothers. Their mothers are none but those who gave birth to them. And indeed, they are saying an objectionable statement and a falsehood. But indeed, Allah is Pardoning and Forgiving.

Interactive Legal Sequencer · Verses 58:3–58:4

Trace the Graduated Expiation (Kaffarah) for Retracting Zihar

If a husband who pronounced zihar wishes to retract his words and resume marital intimacy, Verses 58:3–4 mandate a strict three-tier expiation before they touch one another. Select the husband’s capacity below to inspect which legal tier applies.

Tier I · 58:3
Freeing a Slave (Tahrir Raqabah)

Must be completed before marital intimacy resumes. Channels domestic wrongdoing into human liberation.

Tier II · 58:4
Two Consecutive Months of Fasting

Only if no slave is found. Sixty uninterrupted days of self-restraint before touching one another.

Tier III · 58:4
Feeding Sixty Poor Persons

Only for one genuinely unable to sustain the two-month fast—turning personal contrition into communal nourishment.

Obligatory Ruling (58:3): Emancipation comes first—one cannot skip directly to fasting or feeding if a bonded person can be freed. Verse 58:3 concludes: “That is what you are admonished thereby; and Allah is Acquainted with what you do.”
Movement II · Verses 58:5–58:10

The Unseen Fourth: Omniscience & Najwa

Notice the profound theological bridge between Movement I and Movement II. In 58:1, Allah’s all-encompassing hearing comforts an oppressed woman whispering her grief. In 58:6–7, that exact same omniscience confronts conspirators whispering behind closed doors. Every deed is recorded—“Allah had enumerated it, while they forgot it” (58:6)—and no private conference is ever secluded from His knowledge.

Verse 58:7 · Knowledge Without Boundary 4th of 3

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ يَعْلَمُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ ۖ مَا يَكُونُ مِنْ نَجْوَىٰ ثَلَاثَةٍ إِلَّا هُوَ رَابِعُهُمْ وَلَا خَمْسَةٍ إِلَّا هُوَ سَادِسُهُمْ وَلَا أَدْنَىٰ مِنْ ذَٰلِكَ وَلَا أَكْثَرَ إِلَّا هُوَ مَعَهُمْ أَيْنَ مَا كَانُوا ۖ ثُمَّ يُنَبِّئُهُمْ بِمَا عَمِلُوا يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ

“There is in no private conversation three but that He is the fourth of them, nor are there five but that He is the sixth of them — and no less than that and no more except that He is with them [in knowledge] wherever they are.”

Notice the careful precision of the translation: Allah is with them in knowledge wherever they are, and will inform them of what they did on the Day of Resurrection. Secret counsel is never private from the Creator; only its moral direction separates sin from righteousness.

Forbidden Najwa · Verses 58:8 & 58:10

Conspiracy, Sin & Psychological Harm

Verse 58:8 exposes those who were forbidden from malicious secret meetings yet return to them, conversing about sin (al-ithm), aggression (al-‘udwan), and disobedience to the Messenger (ma‘siyat ar-Rasul)—even greeting the Prophet with distorted words and inwardly mocking, “Why does Allah not punish us for what we say?”

Verse 58:10 unmasks the intent behind such exclusionary whispering: “Private conversation is only from Satan that he may grieve those who have believed” (li-yahzuna). Yet believers are anchored in tawakkul: Satan cannot harm them at all except by Allah’s permission.

Redemptive Najwa · Verse 58:9

Righteousness, Piety & Accountability

Islam does not ban private conversation wholesale; it redirects it. Addressing believers directly (“Ya ayyuha alladhina amanu”), Verse 58:9 forbids private counsel around sin, aggression, and disobedience, and commands instead: “converse about righteousness and piety” (al-birr wat-taqwa).

Discreet consultation is noble when it protects dignity, arranges quiet charity, or resolves conflict—guided by awareness of “Allah, to whom you will be gathered.”

Movement III · Verses 58:11–58:13

Generosity in Assemblies & Discipline in Counsel

Making Room for Others — And Being Raised by Degrees (58:11)

After addressing secretive cliques, Verse 58:11 reforms how believers inhabit shared public space (al-majalis). When told, “Space yourselves” (tafassahu), believers are commanded to widen the circle willingly. The reward mirrors the act: “Allah will make space for you” (yafsahillahu lakum)—opening ease in hearts, sustenance, and Paradise.

Likewise, when told “Arise” (unshuzu)—whether to yield seats to elders, scholars, or urgent communal needs, or to disperse respectfully when a session closes—complying without ego brings elevation: “Allah will raise those who have believed among you and those who were given knowledge, by degrees (darajat).”

The Pedagogical Pause on Private Consultations (58:12–13)

Verses 58:12–13 preserve a remarkable legislative sequence. Initially, believers wishing a private consultation with the Messenger were instructed to offer a charity (sadaqah) beforehand—described as “better for you and purer” (khayrun lakum wa athar), curbing trivial monopolizing of the Prophet’s time while benefiting the poor, with explicit mercy for those lacking financial means.

When believers hesitated (“Have you feared to present before your consultation charities?”, 58:13), Allah relieved the prerequisite (taba ‘alaykum) and pivoted immediately to the enduring anchors of community life: establish prayer (as-salah), give zakah, and obey Allah and His Messenger.

Verse 58:11 · Assembly Etiquette & Knowledge Darajat

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا قِيلَ لَكُمْ تَفَسَّحُوا فِي الْمَجَالِسِ فَافْسَحُوا يَفْسَحِ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ ۖ وَإِذَا قِيلَ انْشُزُوا فَانْشُزُوا يَرْفَعِ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنْكُمْ وَالَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْعِلْمَ دَرَجَاتٍ ۚ وَاللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ خَبِيرٌ

O you who have believed, when you are told, “Space yourselves” in assemblies, then make space; Allah will make space for you. And when you are told, “Arise,” then arise; Allah will raise those who have believed among you and those who were given knowledge, by degrees. And Allah is Acquainted with what you do.

Movement IV · Verses 58:14–58:22

The Party of Satan vs. Hizbullah

The final movement brings the Surah’s underlying conflict into full light. Verses 58:14–18 dissect a fractured identity: people who befriend groups under Allah’s anger while claiming solidarity with believers—“They are neither of you nor of them” (58:14). They swear false oaths knowingly, turning their oaths into a protective shield (junnah, 58:16) to obstruct others from the path of Allah. Even on the Day of Resurrection, habit overrides reality as they attempt to swear falsely before Allah Himself (58:18).

Verses 58:19–22 crystallize this trajectory into two opposing camps: Hizb ash-Shaytan (the Party of Satan) and Hizbullah (the Party of Allah). Notice the defining threshold in Verse 58:22: authentic faith cannot coexist with affectionate alliance toward those who actively oppose (hadda) Allah and His Messenger—even if they are fathers, sons, brothers, or kindred. Conversely, Allah writes faith directly into the hearts of Hizbullah, strengthens them with a spirit from Him, and seals the chapter with mutual delight: “Allah is pleased with them, and they are pleased with Him.”

Important nuance in 58:22: How does this relate to kindness toward non-Muslim relatives?

Look carefully at the exact wording of Verse 58:22: the verse specifies “those who oppose (hadda) Allah and His Messenger”—active hostility and confrontation against faith and the prophetic mission, mirroring yuhadduna Allaha wa Rasulah in 58:5 and 58:20. It forbids affectionate allegiance to hostile opposition, not ordinary justice, mercy, or respectful familial duty toward peaceful relatives.

Verse 58:22 · The Seal of Hizbullah Al-Muflihun · The Successful

لَا تَجِدُ قَوْمًا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ يُوَادُّونَ مَنْ حَادَّ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ وَلَوْ كَانُوا آبَاءَهُمْ أَوْ أَبْنَاءَهُمْ أَوْ إِخْوَانَهُمْ أَوْ عَشِيرَتَهُمْ ۚ أُولَٰئِكَ كَتَبَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ الْإِيمَانَ وَأَيَّدَهُمْ بِرُوحٍ مِنْهُ ۖ وَيُدْخِلُهُمْ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا ۚ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمْ وَرَضُوا عَنْهُ ۚ أُولَٰئِكَ حِزْبُ اللَّهِ ۚ أَلَا إِنَّ حِزْبَ اللَّهِ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ

You will not find a people who believe in Allah and the Last Day having affection for those who oppose Allah and His Messenger, even if they were their fathers or their sons or their brothers or their kindred. Those — He has decreed within their hearts faith and supported them with spirit from Him. And We will admit them to gardens beneath which rivers flow, wherein they abide eternally. Allah is pleased with them, and they are pleased with Him — those are the party of Allah. Unquestionably, the party of Allah — they are the successful.
