Primary Text

Complete Text of Surah An-Nasr (110:1–3)

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
Bismillāhir-Raḥmānir-Raḥīm
In the name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful.
1
إِذَا جَاءَ نَصْرُ اللَّهِ وَالْفَتْحُ

Idhā jā’a naṣrullāhi wal-fatḥ

When the victory of Allah has come and the conquest,

2
وَرَأَيْتَ النَّاسَ يَدْخُلُونَ فِي دِينِ اللَّهِ أَفْوَاجًا

Wa ra’aytan-nāsa yadkhulūna fī dīnillāhi afwājā

And you see the people entering into the religion of Allah in multitudes,

3
فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَاسْتَغْفِرْهُ ۚ إِنَّهُ كَانَ تَوَّابًا

Fa-sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika wastaghfirh · innahū kāna tawwābā

Then exalt [Him] with praise of your Lord and ask forgiveness of Him. Indeed, He is ever Accepting of repentance.

Linguistic Architecture

Grammar as Theology: One Conditional Arc

In Arabic syntax, Surah An-Nasr is not three disconnected statements—it is a single conditional sentence (jumlah shartiyyah). Verses 1 and 2 set the premise; verse 3 delivers the divine mandate. Click each structural stage below to examine how Arabic grammar shapes its spiritual meaning.

Key Arabic Term Pairings & Semantic Nuance

Arabic Pairing Literal Distinction Theological & Practical Insight
نَصْر (Naṣr) &
فَتْح (Fatḥ) 		Naṣr is timely support against adversity; Fatḥ is the opening of closed doors (specifically Makkah). First comes inner divine aid (naṣr), then outward historical opening (fatḥ). Linking both to Allah prevents triumphalism.
سَبِّحْ (Sabbiḥ) &
حَمْد (Ḥamd) 		Tasbīḥ declares God free of every flaw; Ḥamd praises Him with love and gratitude for perfection. Paired via bi-ḥamdi (“alongside praise”), purifying the mind from doubts during hardship and swelling the heart with awe in victory.
اسْتَغْفِرْهُ (Istaghfirhu) &
تَوَّابًا (Tawwābā) 		Form X istaghfir (“earnestly seek covering/pardon”) paired with intensive tawwāb (“repeatedly turning back”). The human asks once; Allah is Tawwāb—constantly welcoming and accepting return. Even the sinless Prophet models humility at life’s summit.

Thematic Synthesis

Why Peak Success Demands Humility & Istighfar

Human empires respond to conquest with monuments, boasting, and complacency. Surah An-Nasr reverses that instinct entirely. Explore each pillar of the Prophetic response diagram to see how humility, tasbīḥ, and istighfār protect the soul at the moment of achievement.

Two Postures in the Moment of Triumph

Dimension Ego-Driven Victory Surah An-Nasr Ethos
Attribution of Success “My strategy, strength, or perseverance won this.” Naṣrullāh — Victory belongs to Allah; humans are merely stewards of effort.
Treatment of Former Adversaries Retaliation, spectacle, and humiliation of the defeated. General amnesty at Makkah’s gates and bowed humility upon the mount.
Inner Emotional State Self-satisfaction, spiritual relaxation, entitlement. Tasbīḥ (awe), Ḥamd (gratitude), and Istighfār (recognizing human shortfall).
