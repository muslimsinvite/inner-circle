Surah An-Nisa

From One Soul to a Just Society — A Complete 176-Verse Study Companion

Chapter 4 opens by anchoring every human relationship in a single origin (nafs wahidah, 4:1) and closes by sealing the final inheritance rule for childless siblings (kalalah, 4:176). Between those bookends, it constructs a complete moral, legal, and spiritual architecture for protecting orphans, reforming marriage and estates, resisting hypocrisy, upholding justice even against oneself, and clarifying prophetic revelation.

Section III · Mirath Lab (Verses 4:11, 4:12 & 4:176)

How Surah An-Nisa Divides an Estate

Before Surah An-Nisa, women and minor children were routinely excluded from inheritance—a practice explicitly overturned in 4:7 (“For men is a share… and for women is a share… be it little or much, an obligatory share”). Verses 4:11, 4:12, and 4:176 establish exact fractions applied only after settling any bequest (wasiyyah) and debts (dayn). Test real scenarios below.

Section IV · Family Law & Social Safeguards

Orphans, Marriage Boundaries & Reconciliation Protocols

Surah An-Nisa treats the household as the proving ground of faith. Rather than leaving vulnerable dependents to custom, verses 4:2–6, 4:19–25, 4:34–36, and 4:127–130 institute transparent guardianship, consent, non-retaliatory divorce ethics, and multi-stage conflict resolution.

4:2–6 & 4:10 · Orphan Stewardship

Test Maturity Before Transfer

Guardians may neither swap inferior property for an orphan’s prime assets (4:2) nor consume their wealth unjustly—warned as eating fire (4:10). Guardians must test orphans until marriageable age and release property upon perceiving sound judgment (rushd), calling witnesses (4:6). Wealthy guardians refrain from fees; needy guardians take only what is equitable.

4:4 & 4:19–21 · Marital Covenant

The Solemn Pledge (Mithaq Ghaliz)

Women cannot be inherited against their will (4:19), nor pressured into forfeiting their bridal gift (saduqat, 4:4). Even if a husband gave a whole treasure (qintar), taking it back upon separation is forbidden (4:20) because spouses have taken a “solemn covenant” (mithaqan ghalizan, 4:21).

4:35 & 4:128–130 · Conflict Resolution

Arbitration & Dignified Parting

When marital discord (shiqaq) threatens collapse, families appoint one arbitrator from his side and one from hers to seek reconciliation (4:35). If a wife fears estrangement, mutual settlement (sulh) is encouraged (4:128). If reconciliation fails, God promises to enrich each separately from His abundance (4:130).

Section V · Governance, Moral Courage & Creed

Justice Without Partiality & Truth Without Excess

The middle and final thirds of Surah An-Nisa move outward from the home to the public square: how a community judges disputes, handles rumors during war, protects non-combatants offering peace, confronts double-minded hypocrisy, and engages Jews and Christians on revelation and the nature of Jesus.

4:58–59 & 4:135 · Pillar Verses of Justice
Verse 4:135 — Witness Against Oneself
يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُونُوا قَوَّامِينَ بِالْقِسْطِ شُهَدَاءَ لِلَّهِ وَلَوْ عَلَىٰ أَنْفُسِكُمْ أَوِ الْوَالِدَيْنِ وَالْأَقْرَبِينَ

“O you who have believed, be persistently standing firm in justice, witnesses for Allah, even if it be against yourselves or parents and relatives. Whether one is rich or poor, Allah is more worthy of both.”

Notice the sequence across the surah: 4:58 commands returning trusts and judging fairly; 4:105–112 forbids defending a deceptive insider at the expense of an innocent outsider; and 4:135 demands testifying truthfully even against oneself, one’s parents, or wealth bias.

4:153–175 · Prophetic Continuity & Christology
Verse 4:171 — “Do Not Commit Excess”
إِنَّمَا الْمَسِيحُ عِيسَى ابْنُ مَرْيَمَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ وَكَلِمَتُهُ أَلْقَاهَا إِلَىٰ مَرْيَمَ وَرُوحٌ مِنْهُ

“The Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary, was but a messenger of Allah and His word which He directed to Mary and a soul [created at a command] from Him. So believe in Allah and His messengers. And do not say, ‘Three’; desist—it is better for you.”

Surah An-Nisa rejects two extremes regarding Jesus: the slander against Mary and claim of killing the Messiah (4:156–157), and the theological excess of Trinity or divine sonship (4:171–172). Instead, 4:163–165 situates Muhammad within the single unbroken chain of Noah, Abraham, Moses, David, and Jesus.
