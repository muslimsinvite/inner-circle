Exegetical & Theological Note

02 · Foundations of Pure Monotheism

Two movements define Tawhid: affirmation and absolute transcendence

Classical theologians observe that Surah Al-Ikhlas balances what must be affirmed about the Creator (verses 1–2) with what must be categorically negated (verses 3–4)—leaving no ambiguity between the Creator and creation.

Verses 112:1–2 · Affirmation (Ithbat)

Who God Is in Essence and Relation

  • Oneness of Essence (Al-Ahad): Indivisible, unique, and uncomposed—not one member within a class of deities, nor made of parts that could separate.
  • Absolute Self-Sufficiency (As-Samad): Complete perfection needing neither sustenance, partner, nor advisor, while every creature turns toward Him in total dependence.
  • Personal Directness (Huwa Allah): The pronoun Huwa (“He”) anchors a direct response to questions about the nature of the Divine: Allah is the proper name of the one true God.
Verses 112:3–4 · Negation (Nafy / Tanzih)

What God Transcends Absolutely

  • No Offspring (Lam yalid): Nothing emanates from Him as a child, subordinate deity, or biological extension; divinity is not shared or transmitted.
  • No Origin (Wa lam yulad): He is uncreated, eternal without a beginning, and preceded by nothing—refuting any mythology of divine birth or succession.
  • No Equivalent (Kufuwan ahad): Neither in essence, attributes, nor action does any being stand as an equal, match, or rival to Him across all time.

03 · Precision of Divine Attributes

Why the Quran chose Al-Ahad and As-Samad

Both attributes carry rare grammatical forms. Neither is a casual synonym for “one” or “eternal”; each shuts down loopholes that ordinary adjectives would leave open.

Term Arabic Grammar & Usage Theological Distinction
Al-Ahad أَحَدٌ Intensive qualitative noun (from ʾ-ḥ-d). In affirmative divine attribution, unique to 112:1; elsewhere mainly used in negations (“not a single one”). Denotes qualitative uniqueness and indivisibility. Cannot take a plural, cannot be part of a sequence, and implies zero likeness.
Al-Wahid وَاحِدٌ Active-participle numerical adjective (from w-ḥ-d). Common across counting and paired with “two, three…” in ordinary Arabic. Denotes numerical oneness (one as opposed to two or many). Because “one” can begin a series, Al-Ahad adds deeper exclusivity.
As-Samad الصَّمَدُ Definite nominal attribute (al- + faʿal pattern from ṣ-m-d), appearing only once in the entire Quran—here at 112:2. Unites total self-sufficiency (needing nothing, solid without flaw) with universal refuge (the sovereign to whom all beings turn).
Lam + Jussive لَمْ يَلِدْ / لَمْ يُولَدْ Particle of past/timeless absolute negation (lam) governing imperfect jussive verbs (active yalid, passive yulad). Refutes past, continuous, or conceivable occurrence—not merely denying present offspring (la) or future intent (lan), but ruling it out across reality.
◆ Four Classical Layers of As-Samad ◆
Layer 01 · Refuge

The Destination of All Need

In classical Arabic, ṣamada ilayhi means “to direct oneself toward someone in urgent need.” Creation perpetually relies on Him; He relies on none.

Layer 02 · Sovereignty

The Master Beyond Equal

Early lexicographers defined As-Samad as the supreme lord (As-Sayyid) whose authority is complete, above whom there is no higher arbiter.

Layer 03 · Permanence

The Undiminished & Eternal

Derived from solid, unhollowed rock, the root evokes absolute fullness and permanence: unlike mortal beings that eat, sleep, age, or break apart, He endures unchanged.

Layer 04 · Bridge

Pivot Between 112:1 and 112:3

Because God is As-Samad—wholly self-sufficient—He needs no heir to carry His legacy (lam yalid) and had no parent to bring Him into existence (lam yulad).

04 · Architectural & Phonetic Symmetry

A four-verse ring sealed by Ahad at both ends

Surah Al-Ikhlas is engineered like a courtyard arch: its opening affirmation (Ahad) and closing negation (kufuwan ahad) form an outer frame around the central hinge of self-sufficiency (As-Samad) and timeless transcendence.
