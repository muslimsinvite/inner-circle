Section 03 · Verses 54:17, 22, 32, 40 & 51

The Fourfold Refrain: “Is There Any Who Will Remember?”

Four times—after Nuh, ’Ad, Thamud, and Lut—the Surah pauses its historical narrative with an identical call, pairing effortless accessibility (yassarna) with an open invitation to active moral reflection (muddakir).

Repeated at 54:17 · 54:22 · 54:32 · 54:40

وَلَقَدْ يَسَّرْنَا الْقُرْآنَ لِلذِّكْرِ فَهَلْ مِنْ مُدَّكِرٍ

“And We have certainly made the Qur’an easy for remembrance, so is there any who will remember?”

1. Yassarna (يَسَّرْنَا) — Made Easy

Facilitated for recitation, memorization, comprehension, and drawing practical moral lessons—removing the excuse of obscurity.

2. Muddakir (مُدَّكِرٍ) — One Who Remembers

An intensive form meaning one who actively receives warning, learns from the past, and turns back before settlement arrives. Note the fifth echo at 54:51: “And We have already destroyed your kinds, so is there any who will remember?”

Section 04 · Verses 54:43–54:53

Dismantling False Security & Cosmic Decree

Turning directly to the Surah’s contemporaries, these verses shatter every illusion of immunity—scriptural privilege, numerical strength, or unrecorded deeds—before anchoring reality in God’s singular, instantaneous command.

Three Pillars of False Security Dismantled

1. Presumed Exemption · 54:43

“Are your disbelievers better than those [former ones], or have you immunity in the scripture (bara’atun fiz-zubur)?” Neither lineage nor past privilege grants sanctuary from moral accountability.

2. Collective Solidarity · 54:44–46

Their boast—“We are an assembly supporting [each other]” (jami’un muntasir)—collapses twice: earthly defeat in retreat (54:45), followed by the Hour itself, “more disastrous and more bitter” (54:46).

3. Unaccounted Deeds · 54:49–53

All things are created with exact measure (bi-qadar, 54:49), God’s command executes “like a glance of the eye” (ka-lamhin bil-basar, 54:50), and every small and great deed is inscribed in written records (az-zubur, 54:52–53).

Section 05 · Verses 54:54–54:55

The Seat of Honor Near an Omnipotent Sovereign

After storms, floods, blasts, and the terror of Saqar, the final two verses bring the Surah to complete stillness—bookending the divine name Muqtadir from judgment (54:42) to eternal nearness (54:55).

Culmination · 54:54–54:55

إِنَّ الْمُتَّقِينَ فِي جَنَّاتٍ وَنَهَرٍ ۝ فِي مَقْعَدِ صِدْقٍ عِنْدَ مَلِيكٍ مُقْتَدِرٍ

“Indeed, the righteous will be among gardens and rivers, in a seat of honor near a Sovereign, Perfect in Ability.”

Jannatin wa Nahar (Gardens & Rivers)

Notice the striking reversal of water imagery: for Noah’s deniers, water brought drowning flood (54:11–12); for the righteous (al-muttaqin), flowing rivers nourish tranquil gardens (54:54).

Maq’adi Sidq ‘Inda Malikin Muqtadir

A “seat of honor/truth” (maq’adi sidq) free of vanity or falsehood, defined not only by place but by proximity (‘inda) to the Sovereign (Malik) whose absolute capability (Muqtadir) earlier seized Pharaoh (54:42).
