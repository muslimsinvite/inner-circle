Surah Sad: Pride, Trial, and Sincere Return

Opening with an oath by the Qur'an of reminder and diagnosing human disbelief as arrogant self-importance ('izzah) and fracture (shiqaq), Chapter 38 unfolds as a mirror between two ways of standing before God: the unyielding pride of Iblis and past empires, versus the instant, trembling repentance (inabah) of Prophets Dawud, Sulaiman, and Ayyub.

Section 02 · Prophetic Case Studies (38:17–48)

How Awwab Servants Turn Trials Into Nearness

Commanded to be patient over what the disbelievers say (38:17), the Prophet Muhammad is told to remember three exemplary servants—Dawud, Sulaiman, and Ayyub. Each receives a distinct test involving power, beauty, or physical pain, and each responds with the exact same spiritual reflex: immediate return (inabah) to Allah.

Prophet & Verses Divine Gift / Station The Specific Trial (Fitnah) Immediate Response (Inabah) Covenant & Outcome
Dawud (David)
38:17–26		 Possessor of strength (dha al-ayd); mountains & birds praising; strengthened kingdom, wisdom & decisive speech. Two adversaries climb the prayer-chamber wall over 99 ewes vs. 1 ewe; Dawud judges quickly before hearing both sides fully. Realized Allah tried him; sought forgiveness (istaghfara), fell down bowing in prostration (kharra raki'an), and repented (anaba). Forgiven, granted nearness (zulfa) & good return (husn ma'ab); appointed khalifah to judge by truth, not desire (hawa).
Sulaiman (Solomon)
38:30–40		 Gift to Dawud; "an excellent servant, repeatedly turning back"; later given command of wind and jinn builders & divers. Poised racehorses distracted him from remembrance until sunset (38:31–33); later a body (jasad) placed upon his throne (38:34). Returned (anaba), corrected the attachment to the horses, and prayed for forgiveness and a unique kingdom from al-Wahhab. Wind & jinn subjected without account (bi-ghayri hisab); promised exact same nearness (zulfa) & good return (husn ma'ab).
Ayyub (Job)
38:41–44		 Exemplary patient servant (sabiran, ni'ma al-'abd), repeatedly turning back (awwab). Touched by Satan with severe hardship (nusb) and torment ('adhab); bound by a painful personal oath. Called gently to his Lord naming his distress without complaint against Allah; obeyed the command to strike the earth. Cool healing spring, family restored & doubled as mercy, and gentle oath fulfillment with a bundle of grass (dighthan).
Theological Core · 38:27–29 & 38:45–48

Creation Has Purpose—And the Prophetic Chorus Lives for the Hereafter

Between Dawud and Sulaiman stands a pivotal declaration: Allah did not create heaven and earth aimlessly (batilan, 38:27), nor are the righteous equal to the wicked. The Qur'an itself is a blessed Book revealed so that "those of understanding would reflect upon its verses" (38:29). After Ayyub, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob are praised as possessors of strength and vision (uli al-aydi wa-l-absar) chosen for one exclusive trait: remembrance of the eternal Home (dhikra al-dar, 38:46), alongside Ishmael, Elisha, and Dhul-Kifl among the outstanding (al-akhyar).

Section 03 · The Cosmic Origin of Pride (38:69–85)

Adam's Clay, Iblis's Fire, and the Anatomy of Istikbar

Earlier in the chapter, Allah asked why Quraysh refused the Reminder out of 'izzah (38:2) and warned of Hell's bitter mutual blaming (takhasum, 38:64). In the finale, Surah Sad uncovers the ancestral prototype of that refusal: Iblis in the exalted assembly (al-mala' al-a'la). Step through the five dramatic beats of 38:71–85.

Dimension The Way of Iblis & the Confederates The Way of the Prophets & the Mukhlasin
Posture Before Command Istakbara — inflated self-worth; refusal to prostrate (38:74). Kharra raki'an / Sajidin — immediate bowing and obedience (38:24, 38:73).
Criterion of Value Surface comparison & ego: "I am better than him — fire over clay" (38:76). Divine honor & accountability: breathed spirit (38:72), Day of Account (38:26).
Response to Misstep Blame-shifting and defiance—even swearing by Allah's Might to corrupt others (38:82). Self-reckoning, seeking forgiveness, and turning straight back (anaba, 38:24, 38:34).
Final Place of Return Sharr ma'ab — mutual cursing and contention in Hell (38:55–64). Husn ma'ab — nearness (zulfa) and Gardens of perpetual residence (38:25, 49–54).
