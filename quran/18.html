Trial 1 · Faith under pressure 18:9–26 · 18 verses

Companions of the Cave & the Inscription (Ashab al-Kahf)

Young believers (فِتْيَةٌ) stood up before an idolatrous community, refused to invoke any deity besides the Lord of the heavens and earth, and retreated to a cave where Allah sheltered them in sleep. Toggle the states below to examine how the text describes their preservation and awakening.

    Trial 2 · Wealth and pride 18:32–46 · 15 verses

    The Two Men, Two Gardens & Worldly Impermanence

    Given two flourishing gardens of grapevines bordered by palms, crops, and a gushing river, one man equated abundance with permanence and superiority—until ruin exposed how fleeting worldly adornment is compared with enduring good deeds (18:46).

    The Owner's Delusion (18:34–36)

    Self-sufficiency & denial

    • 18:34 · Boast of status "I am greater than you in wealth and mightier in [numbers of] men."
    • 18:35 · Illusion of permanence Entering his garden unjust to himself, he said: "I do not think that this will perish — ever."
    • 18:36 · Presumption over the Hereafter "And I do not think the Hour will occur. And even if I should be brought back to my Lord, I will surely find better than this as a return."
    The Companion's Counsel (18:37–41)

    Origin, gratitude & dependence

    • 18:37–38 · Remembering origin "Have you disbelieved in He who created you from dust and then from a sperm-drop and then proportioned you as a man? But as for me, He is Allah, my Lord."
    • 18:39 · Words of humility "Why did you not say, مَا شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ — What Allah willed [has occurred]; there is no power except in Allah?"
    • 18:40–41 · Fragility of earthly blessing Warns that Allah could turn the garden into smooth, dusty ground or sink its water beyond reach.

    Practical retention

    Six anchoring phrases that govern daily life

    Surah Al-Kahf translates its stories into concrete habits of speech, companionship, and sincerity. Review these six verbatim expressions from the chapter.

    Verse Arabic expression Meaning in the source Principle taught
    18:10 رَبَّنَا آتِنَا مِنْ لَدُنْكَ رَحْمَةً وَهَيِّئْ لَنَا مِنْ أَمْرِنَا رَشَدًا "Our Lord, grant us from Yourself mercy and prepare for us from our affair right guidance." Seeking mercy and sound direction before action
    18:23–24 إِلَّا أَنْ يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ Never say "I will do that tomorrow" except when adding, "If Allah wills." Humility regarding future plans and remembrance when forgetting
    18:39 مَا شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ "What Allah willed [has occurred]; there is no power except in Allah." Protecting the heart against pride when blessed with wealth
    18:46 وَالْبَاقِيَاتُ الصَّالِحَاتُ خَيْرٌ عِنْدَ رَبِّكَ ثَوَابًا "The enduring good deeds are better to your Lord for reward and better for [one's] hope." Valuing lasting deeds over fleeting earthly adornment
    18:109 لَنَفِدَ الْبَحْرُ قَبْلَ أَنْ تَنْفَدَ كَلِمَاتُ رَبِّي "If the sea were ink for [writing] the words of my Lord, the sea would be exhausted before the words of my Lord were exhausted." Awe at the boundless knowledge and speech of Allah
    18:110 فَلْيَعْمَلْ عَمَلًا صَالِحًا وَلَا يُشْرِكْ بِعِبَادَةِ رَبِّهِ أَحَدًا "So whoever would hope for the meeting with his Lord — let him do righteous work and not associate in the worship of his Lord anyone." The twin pillars of acceptance: righteous action and pure sincerity
